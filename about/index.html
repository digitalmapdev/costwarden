Why we exist

Overcharges don't announce themselves. We built something that finds them anyway.

CostWarden started from a simple observation: the money a business loses to overpayments and billing errors is almost never visible on a single invoice. It's visible across thousands of them — which is exactly the kind of problem software is good at, and people aren't.

The problem we saw

Finance and procurement teams already know, roughly, that some money is leaking out through supplier overcharges, duplicate payments and contracts nobody's re-checked in years. What they don't have is time to read every invoice line by line to find it — so a manual audit samples a subset, and whatever's outside that sample goes uncaught, quarter after quarter.

That gap — between "we probably have some leakage" and "here's exactly where, and what it's worth" — is the gap CostWarden was built to close.

What we believe

Four rules we don't bend.

01

Every line, not a sample.

If software can read every invoice instead of a handful, it should — that's where the smaller, easy-to-miss overpayments hide.

02

Findings should be provable.

A number with no source line behind it is an opinion, not a finding. Every result traces back to the invoice it came from.

03

Start free, prove it on real data.

We'd rather show you a real, quantified number from your own spend export than ask you to trust a sales pitch.

04

No fabricated certainty.

Where we show an estimate — like the example figures on our homepage — we say so plainly, instead of dressing up an illustration as a guarantee.
